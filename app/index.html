Decentralized, open-source voting protocol on the Base blockchain

Vote on public decisions, privately

We the sovereign people, on the record.

Promissa Protocol is an open, decentralized way to vote, petition, and track public decisions until they're done. Everything is recorded on Base, a public blockchain: results, proposals and every change of power are open for anyone to check, and no single company (including us) can quietly change or delete them. How you voted stays secret.

No app store, no wallet, no crypto. Your phone or computer unlocks a private voter key with Face ID, your fingerprint, or your device PIN.

Citizens' assemblies

A small group of residents, drawn at random from the voter list like a jury, studies a question with experts and recommends what everyone then votes on. Nobody can be bought or pressured before the draw, because nobody knows who will be chosen. The draw uses a number nobody knew in advance, and anyone can redo it to check it.

Announce a draw

The voter list is fixed when you announce. The draw then uses the code of a block made after that moment, which nobody can know or choose in advance. Five times as many people as seats are put in line, so declined seats pass to the next person.

Civic budget

Decide together how to spend part of the public budget. Everyone gets the same credits each round; nobody can buy more, sell them or see how you spent yours.

    Set up a whole organization

    Or set up one body at a time. Each body sets its own rules; later changes never affect items already on the agenda.

    Seats

    Roles and alternates

    Chair: runs the agenda and can withdraw items before voting. Clerk: publishes the agenda, records votes taken in the room, records closed sessions. Staff: drafts items for members to sponsor. Roles can end on a date. Self-governed bodies can also vote on these themselves.

    Paper ballots and in-person results

    Record counts from polling places, precincts or meetings. One clerk records, another confirms; a photo of the signed tally sheet goes with it. For a Promissa poll, paper voters check in on their phones first, so the paper register matches the voter list and nobody is counted twice.
