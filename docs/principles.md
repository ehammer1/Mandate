# Founding principles

Mandate borrows its design from the ideas behind the US Constitution. This page maps each
principle to how Mandate puts it into practice, and names the gaps still to close.

| Principle | Where it comes from | In Mandate | Gap |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Consent of the governed** | Declaration of Independence; "We the People" (Preamble) | Decisions are made by residents' votes; mandates exist only because a vote created them | The council is appointed, not elected |
| **Separation of powers** | Articles I–III | Residents set rules and goals (votes, charter); responsible parties carry out mandates; independent verifiers judge whether they were met; the council only manages roles and can't set rules or create mandates | The council also approves upgrades of the contracts themselves |
| **Checks and balances** | Federalist No. 51: "ambition must be made to counteract ambition" | Council: several approvals, a public waiting period, any member can cancel. Independent watchdog. Cross-checked polls. Verifier thresholds. Budgets held in escrow until verified | Watchdogs should be run by several independent organisations |
| **A written constitution, rule of law** | Article VI | The charter: the voting rules are public, enforced by the contracts, with fixed outer limits | Some protections should be beyond any vote (see entrenched rights) |
| **Amendment by supermajority** | Article V | Rule changes need a longer vote, voters with full rights and a 51–90% supermajority | Contract upgrades are approved by the council, not ratified by the public |
| **Protecting minorities from the majority** | Federalist No. 10 | Supermajorities for rules; limits no vote can exceed; secret ballots | No list of rights that a majority can never vote away |
| **No retroactive rules** | Article I, Section 9 (ex post facto) | Each poll freezes its voter list, weights and supermajority when it opens | None |
| **Equal protection, one person one vote** | 14th Amendment; *Reynolds v. Sims* (1964) | One voter code per person; equal civic credits for everyone | Weighting ID-checked votes above others must not be used to elect public officials; keep it for advisory and internal decisions |
| **Due process** | 5th and 14th Amendments | Removals are on the public record | No notice, stated reason or appeal before a member is removed |
| **Petition and free speech** | 1st Amendment | Anyone can propose a poll by posting a bond; all text is public and permanent | A bond can price people out; petitions should also be possible by gathering signatures |
| **Public accounts** | Article I, Section 9: "a regular Statement and Account of the Receipts and Expenditures of all public Money" | Every vote, budget, escrow and change is on a public ledger, with a live dashboard | None |
| **A journal of proceedings** | Article I, Section 5 | The notice board keeps questions, evidence and reasons permanently; the watchdog keeps a public history | None |
| **Removal from office** | Article II, Section 4 (impeachment) | The council can remove clerks and verifiers instantly once approved | The public can't yet recall a council member, verifier or responsible party by vote |
| **Rotation in office** | Articles of Confederation (term limits); Jefferson's "rotation" | — | No terms for council members or verifiers |
| **Federalism and local control** | 10th Amendment | Each community runs its own charter, voter list and council (see `scaling-plan.md`) | Layers (city, county, state) don't yet connect |
| **Conflicts of interest** | Emoluments clauses | A responsible party can't verify their own mandate | Council members can still approve changes that benefit themselves |

## Next steps that close the gaps

1. **An elected council with terms and recall**: residents add and remove council members
   by binding vote, terms expire, and any member can be recalled.
2. **Entrenched rights**: a short list no vote can change (secret ballot, one person one
   vote, equal civic credits, the right to see every record), and contract upgrades only
   after a public ratification vote.
3. **Due process for removals**: notice to the member, a reason on the public record, and
   an appeal window before a removal takes effect (except for proven fraud).
4. **Citizen initiatives by signature**: residents put a question on the ballot by
   gathering enough anonymous signatures, with no money required.
